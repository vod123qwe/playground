# 👹 BOSS Scene: Defend a design decision

**Mode:** 🎙️ role-play · **Skill:** Register + Fluency under pressure · **Difficulty:** Nightmare

## Setup
A client challenges a decision you made. They're not hostile, but they push back.
Your job: stay calm, acknowledge, reframe, justify — without freezing or getting
defensive. This is a BOSS: it combines everything.

## 🎧 Input (model — how a confident designer handles pushback)
> Client: "Why did you go with tokens instead of just hardcoding the values? It
> feels like over-engineering."
> You: "That's a fair point, and it does add some upfront work. The way I'd frame
> it is this: tokens give us a single source of truth, so when your brand color
> changes, we update one value instead of hunting through forty screens. There's a
> trade-off — a bit more setup now — but it pays off the first time you rebrand."

## 🎙️ Your turn
The coach (me) plays the skeptical client and will push back 3 times. Use the
**acknowledge → reframe → justify** pattern. Don't rush; "That's a fair point"
buys you thinking time.

## Target chunks
- "That's a fair point. The way I'd frame it is…"
- "We deliberately chose X over Y because…"
- "There's a trade-off here: …"
- 🆘 if you freeze: "Bear with me for a second…"

## 🔥 Difficulty scaling
- **Nightmare:** 3 polite pushbacks.
- **Hell:** rapid-fire interruptions; client talks fast and over you.
